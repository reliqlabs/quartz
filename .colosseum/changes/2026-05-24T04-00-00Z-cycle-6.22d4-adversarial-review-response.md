## Why docstring rather than type-enforcement

The `[NeZero n]` typeclass route requires every consumer of `commitHash`, `commitHashBytes`, `pkOfUserData`, `tdxVerifier`, `verifyTdxQuote`, `userDataOf`, `mrEnclaveOf`, and the lift theorems to declare `[NeZero n]` (or thread `(hn : 0 < n)` explicitly). The cascade hits ~20 files and ~80 declaration sites. In the in-fork closure, no consumer ever instantiates these at `n = 0` — the parameter is uniformly threaded as a security parameter, never bound. The realistic threat model is "a careless caller writes `commitHashE 0` and exploits the resulting `False`", which requires deliberate misuse of an internal Lean function.

The honest tradeoff: structural type-enforcement is the right end state (rules out misuse mechanically); docstring-convention is the pragmatic interim (rules out misuse by convention, much less work, equivalent for the in-fork audit story). The adversarial review's finding #1/#14 stands — the axiom family IS constructively-False at `n = 0` — but no closure of any in-tree theorem reaches that derivation.

A future cycle can re-attempt the type-enforcement (`[NeZero n]` typeclass cascade) as a methodical mechanical refactor when the time investment is justified.

## Methodology meta-finding

The cycle-6.22.d.3 wholesale parameterisation surfaced a class of bugs that the prior single-axiom shape never had: family-of-axioms-at-degenerate-parameter inconsistencies. The single `commitHashE : UserDataCommit ↪ UserData(512)` axiom required abstract pigeonhole reasoning to derive `False`; the parameterised family `commitHashE (n) : UserDataCommit ↪ UserData n` admits constructive `False` at small `n`.

This is a general pattern: **parameterising an abstract injectivity axiom by a security parameter `n` introduces inconsistency at every `n` where `2^n < |source|`**. The fix discipline is to either bound `n` from below at the axiom level (type-enforced or by convention) or to move the axiom out of the closure entirely (RO model, cycle 6.22 path).

Add to colosseum methodology v0.4 ask candidates: **when parameterising an abstract injectivity/embedding axiom by a security parameter, audit the family for degenerate-parameter inconsistencies and either type-enforce the lower bound or document the safe-use convention prominently at the axiom site.** The adversarial-review surface time invested in cycle 6.22.d.4 would have been cheaper to invest pre-emptively during the cycle-6.22.d.3 parameterisation design.
